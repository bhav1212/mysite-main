13:T11f5,<p>A team adds a knowledge graph to its retrieval system and expects better answers. The graph creates new possibilities: relationships, communities, and multi-hop exploration. It also creates new errors when entities or relationships are extracted incorrectly.</p>
<p>A graph is a representation, not a source of truth. Before choosing GraphRAG, decide which question cannot be answered well by the existing lookup or retrieval process and what evidence a correct answer requires.</p>
<h2 id="section-1">Local lookup and global synthesis are different</h2>
<p>The <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130">original GraphRAG paper</a> focuses on query-oriented summarization over a corpus, using extracted entities and relationships, graph communities, and community summaries. Its motivation includes global questions such as the main themes across a large document collection.</p>
<p>That is different from “what is the approved expiry date for this contract?” A global summary may be valuable for theme discovery and poor as the sole evidence for an exact field. Keep direct source lookup available rather than forcing every question through a community report.</p>
<h2 id="section-2">Separate the graph from the evidence ledger</h2>
<p>In a synthetic technology-scouting corpus, store a claim, its source passage, date, entity identifiers, and relation type. An edge saying “supplier uses method” should point to evidence that supports that particular relationship. A co-occurrence of the supplier name and method is not necessarily deployment.</p>
<p>Give extracted and verified edges different states. Preserve negation, scope, and uncertainty. “Testing a prototype” should not become “commercially operates.” Entity resolution matters when several companies or products share a name.</p>
<h2 id="section-3">Indexing has an operating cost</h2>
<p>Extraction, graph construction, community detection, and report generation can add preprocessing work and update complexity. A new document may require more than inserting an embedding. Compare the maintenance cost against the benefit for the selected task.</p>
<p>Source deletions and access changes need propagation into graph edges and derived summaries. A summary can leak a restricted fact even after its original document is excluded from retrieval. Permission-aware indexing and answer-time checks are both part of the design.</p>
<h2 id="section-4">Build a task-level comparison</h2>
<p>Use a frozen corpus with exact-lookup, multi-document synthesis, relationship, and unanswerable questions. Compare lexical retrieval, dense or hybrid retrieval, and the graph approach. Predefine source-support and completeness criteria rather than scoring only fluent answers.</p>
<p>Measure correct entity resolution, evidence coverage, unsupported relationship claims, answer usefulness, latency, and total indexing/query cost. Include contradictory sources and a correction to an important entity. A graph win on global themes does not establish a win on all task groups.</p>
<h2 id="section-5">Read the original passage at the decision boundary</h2>
<p>Community summaries are useful retrieval artifacts, but they are lossy syntheses. For a consequential claim, expose the original passage and its qualification. A valid citation to a report does not establish that every statement in the report is accurate.</p>
<p>When the graph suggests a connection, treat it as an investigation lead until the evidence supports the requested inference. This is especially important in foresight, where research attention, prototypes, patents, and commercial adoption are different observations.</p>
<h2 id="section-6">Keep the architecture conditional</h2>
<p>The <a href="https://microsoft.github.io/graphrag/">GraphRAG documentation</a> describes project workflows and options. Pin the implemented version and evaluate your configuration; the name “GraphRAG” covers choices that materially affect outcomes.</p>
<p>Use a graph when it earns its place through task evidence. Preserve a simpler path for questions that need a direct record, and keep graph-derived claims traceable enough to correct. The product benefit is a better-supported answer, not a more elaborate index.</p><h2 id="section-7">Sources and further reading</h2><ul><li><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130">Edge et al.
