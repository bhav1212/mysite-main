<p class="post-typo-sub">Bangkok + an Island, the Honest Route</p>

<p>Thailand is the country where I stopped pretending to be 'off the beaten path'. The beaten path is beaten because it's good. The route below is the one I'd suggest to a first-time visitor with about ten days: a few days of city, a few days of water, one travel day in between.</p>
<h2 id="section-1">Route, day by day</h2>
<p><strong>Days 1–3 — Bangkok.</strong> Grand Palace and Wat Pho on day one (early). Chinatown street-food crawl. A long-tail boat through the Thonburi canals. Don't try to fit the Chatuchak weekend market into a weekday; it isn't there.</p>
<p><strong>Day 4 — Travel south.</strong> Domestic flight from BKK to Krabi (1.5h) or Surat Thani for the Koh Samui ferry. Either way: half-travel day, half-recovery day.</p>
<p><strong>Days 5–8 — Krabi / Railay or Koh Samui.</strong> Pick one. Krabi → Railay Beach is more dramatic (limestone cliffs). Koh Samui is easier (resorts, infrastructure). Day trips to Phi Phi or Ang Thong are touristy but legitimately beautiful.</p>
<p><strong>Day 9 — Return north.</strong> Fly back to Bangkok. A spare evening to revisit a favourite restaurant or rooftop.</p>
<p><strong>Day 10 — Bangkok or Ayutthaya.</strong> If you haven't seen Ayutthaya, do it as a day trip: 90-minute train, ancient temples, back in time for dinner.</p>
<h2 id="section-2">Practical notes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>The taxi rule</strong> in Bangkok: insist on the meter, or use Grab. The flat-rate quote is always a worse deal.</li>
<li><strong>Tuk-tuks</strong> are a one-time experience, not a transit strategy.</li>
<li><strong>Beach booking:</strong> book the boat / ferry through your hotel; the open-counter places at the harbour are markup-heavy.</li>
<li><strong>SIM card:</strong> AIS or TrueMove desks at the airport; 7-day tourist data is cheap and worth it.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="section-3">What I'd write more about</h2>
<ul>
<li>The Koh Samui versus Phuket decision, from someone who's done both.</li>
<li>A guide to Bangkok night markets that aren't aimed at tourists.</li>
<li>Why the long-tail boat at sunrise is worth the early start.</li>
</ul>
<p>

    day with photos, specific places, and longer reflections as I revisit my notes.
