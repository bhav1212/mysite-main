<p>The route below is what I actually did over eleven days in Croatia in November. Off-season meant smaller crowds, slower buses, and a quieter coast. The itinerary stitches together two national parks, three coastal cities, and one overnight bus ride.</p>
<p>Sharing the bones of it here. I'll add longer notes per stop when I have time to write them up properly.</p>
<h2 id="section-1">The route, day by day</h2>
<p><strong>Day 8 (Nov):</strong> Zagreb. Reach + roam the city. Night in Zagreb.</p>
<p><strong>Day 9:</strong> Plitvice National Park during the day; Plitvice → Zagreb → Dubrovnik overnight bus.</p>
<p><strong>Day 10:</strong> Arrive Dubrovnik. Dubrovnik Pass for the city walls and museums. Three nights here.</p>
<p><strong>Days 11–12:</strong> Dubrovnik. Cable car, walls, Old Town, Lokrum if the ferry is running.</p>
<p><strong>Day 13:</strong> Dubrovnik → Split. 8am bus. Coastal road; takes most of the day.</p>
<p><strong>Day 14:</strong> Split city day (Diocletian's Palace area).</p>
<p><strong>Days 15–16:</strong> Trek and day-trip to Trogir from Split.</p>
<p><strong>Day 17:</strong> Split → Krka National Park → Zagreb. Long travel day. Ticket combo: Split→Skradin (boat or bus to Krka), then Skradin→Zagreb.</p>
<p><strong>Day 18:</strong> Zagreb → Baden-Baden. Afternoon flight home.</p>
<h2 id="section-2">Notes that held up</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>The overnight bus from Zagreb to Dubrovnik</strong> saves a night of accommodation, but you trade that for actual sleep. Worth it once.</li>
<li><strong>The Dubrovnik Pass</strong> is the cheapest way to get on the city walls plus several museums in one go.</li>
<li><strong>Plitvice and Krka in November</strong> are noticeably emptier than the summer photos suggest. Some upper trails close for the season; the main loops stay open.</li>
<li><strong>Trogir as a day trip</strong> from Split is small, walled, walkable in a half-day. Good break from Split's denser old town.</li>
<li><strong>Bus connections matter more than flight bookings</strong> in this part of Croatia. The coastal-road buses are reliable; the inland connections (Plitvice, Krka) take research.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="section-3">What I'd write up next</h2>
<p>The interesting stories from this trip don't fit a "best of" list, so I'm saving them for a longer post. A few I'm thinking through:</p>
<ul>
<li>What an overnight bus along the Adriatic actually feels like.</li>
<li>Dubrovnik before and after the cruise ships dock.</li>
<li>Why Krka deserves more attention than it gets.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you're planning a similar route and want detail on any specific stop, send me a note.</p>
