<p class="post-typo-sub">Three Cities in Four Days</p>

<p>Belgium is a country I underestimated until I went. It's small, the trains across it are short, and every city manages to be a real, distinct place. The route below covers the three I'd recommend in order — Brussels first, Bruges middle, Antwerp as a finisher.</p>
<h2 id="section-1">Route, day by day</h2>
<p><strong>Day 1 — Brussels.</strong> Grand-Place at golden hour. The Mont des Arts and the comic-strip route. Dinner around Sainte-Catherine for moules-frites.</p>
<p><strong>Day 2 — Brussels → Bruges.</strong> Hour by train. Bruges in a day is enough; the centre is small. Climb the Belfry, walk the canals, sit in the Markt for a long lunch.</p>
<p><strong>Day 3 — Bruges → Ghent → Antwerp.</strong> Optional half-day in Ghent on the way to Antwerp. Ghent is what Bruges would be if it weren't quite so on the tourist trail.</p>
<p><strong>Day 4 — Antwerp.</strong> Cathedral of Our Lady, MAS museum, the diamond district for a curious walk. Antwerp is the city my designer friends rave about — they're right.</p>
<h2 id="section-2">Practical notes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Trains:</strong> the SNCB Eurail-equivalent week pass pays for itself if you're doing all three.</li>
<li><strong>Languages:</strong> Brussels is French-leaning, Bruges and Ghent are Flemish, Antwerp Flemish with more English. Start with bonjour or goeiedag and the rest is fine.</li>
<li><strong>Beer:</strong> any specialised bar will have 100+ varieties; ask for a Trappist if you want the canonical experience.</li>
<li><strong>Chocolate:</strong> Pierre Marcolini and Neuhaus are the names. Skip the tourist-row shops near the Grand-Place.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="section-3">What I'd write more about</h2>
<ul>
<li>Why Antwerp gets the design-world buzz that Brussels doesn't.</li>
<li>A Trappist-beer day, structured like a tasting menu.</li>
<li>The Bruges-vs-Ghent decision for first-timers.</li>
</ul>
<p>

    day with photos, specific places, and longer reflections as I revisit my notes.
  </p>
